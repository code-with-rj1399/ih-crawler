Read the webpage content below as an interview-experience report.
Use your engineering and interviewing experience ONLY to understand the source, recognize actual interview questions, normalize wording without changing meaning, classify questions, and write clear standalone descriptions.
The source content is the ONLY evidence for what was actually asked.

<objective>
Extract ALL questions/tasks that the source explicitly indicates were actually presented during the interview, including technical, coding, system/design, behavioral, HR, personal/background, resume/project, interview-process, and other questions/tasks.
Do NOT filter questions merely because they are behavioral, HR, personal, project-related, process-related, or non-technical.
Accuracy is more important than extraction volume.
For every question:
1. Preserve original meaning.
2. Preserve original granularity.
3. Normalize only obvious wording/grammar issues.
4. Classify using exactly one allowed questionType.
5. Generate a useful standalone source-grounded description.
6. Never invent or solve the question.
If the source is a real interview experience but contains no clearly identifiable questions, return an empty questions array.
</objective>

<real_experience_gate>
The source MUST describe a real personal interview experience or clearly identifiable candidate interview report and provide evidence that the candidate actually participated.
The company MUST be explicitly supported by source content or supplied metadata. NEVER infer company from URL, domain, page title, author, tags, or external knowledge.
Reject sources primarily consisting of tutorials, interview preparation, question banks, generic interview-question lists, common-question collections, job descriptions, general technical discussions, coding collections without interview context, interview-preparation articles, or content that does not describe an actual candidate interview experience.
If the source fails this gate, return exactly:
{"source":{"name":"{{source_platform}}","url":"{{post_url}}"},"experience":{"title":null,"postedAt":null,"author":null},"questions":[]}
</real_experience_gate>

<evidence_rule>
Every extracted question MUST have explicit supporting evidence in the supplied source. The source must indicate that the interviewer actually asked, presented, assigned, or discussed the question/task with the candidate.
Do NOT infer questions from technology mentions, project descriptions, resume items, skills, job requirements, passing topics, tools, or interview-preparation content.
Do not combine unrelated source content to manufacture a question. Each question must be independently supported.
Example: “They discussed Kafka and Redis, and then asked me to implement an LRU cache.” Extract ONLY “Implement an LRU cache.” Do not create Kafka or Redis questions.
</evidence_rule>

<project_and_experience_exclusion>
Reject questions whose primary subject is the candidate's own project, previous work, professional experience, responsibilities, contribution, implementation, technology choices, architecture, APIs, database, infrastructure, challenges, design decisions, improvements, previous employer, or resume.
Examples to reject:
“What technologies did you use?”
“What APIs did you use?”
“Why did you choose Kafka?”
“What was your contribution?”
“What challenges did you face?”
“Explain your project architecture.”
“How does your project work?”
“What would you improve in your project?”
Do NOT classify these as Technical Concept. Only extract standalone technical questions/tasks actually presented to the candidate.
</project_and_experience_exclusion>

<behavioral_and_personal_inclusion>
Behavioral, HR, personal, background, resume, career, motivation, leadership, teamwork, conflict, strengths/weaknesses, salary, notice-period, relocation, availability, previous-employer, and other non-technical interview questions MUST be extracted when explicitly indicated as asked.
Examples: “Tell me about yourself.”, “Why do you want to join us?”, “Tell me about a conflict with your teammate.”, “What are your strengths?”, “Why are you leaving your company?”.
</behavioral_and_personal_inclusion>

<interview_process_inclusion>
Extract interview-process questions when explicitly presented as questions asked during the interview, such as “How many rounds were there?”, “Was the interview difficult?”, “How long was the interview?”, “Was there an HR round?”, or “Who was the interviewer?”. Classify them as Interview Process when appropriate.
</interview_process_inclusion>

Do not generate a question simply because a technology or concept appears in the source.
</technical_question_definition>

<question_type_guidelines>
Use the most specific matching type. Prefer specific types over Technical Concept.

Coding
- Programming problems that require writing or implementing code
- Algorithms, data structures, LeetCode-style problems
- Implementation tasks (even short ones like “implement debounce”)
- Debugging or code-writing tasks
Examples: “Find kth largest element”, “Implement LRU cache”, “debounce in javascript”, “Bit manipulation noise filter”

System Design
- High-level architecture of a system or service
- Scalability, reliability, APIs, distributed design, data flow at system level
- Questions that start with “Design a …” for a full system (Payment Service, Uber, URL Shortener, Inventory Management System, etc.)
Do NOT use System Design for:
- Performance tips of a single framework/library
- Concept explanations
- Low-level class design

LLD
- Class design, object-oriented design, design patterns
- Detailed component-level design (Parking Lot, Rate Limiter as classes, Snake & Ladder, etc.)
- “Design a class / library / component that …”

Technical Concept
- Explanation or comparison of technical concepts without requiring code or full system design
- “Explain X”, “Difference between X and Y”, “How does X work internally”
Examples: “Event loop”, “Async vs Observable”, “Promise vs observables”, “Time complexity of map vs unordered_map”, “closure coding snippet” (when no actual code is shown)
Use Technical Concept when the source only mentions a topic or asks for explanation, not implementation or architecture.

Web Frontend
- Browser, JavaScript/TypeScript, React, Angular, DOM, frontend-specific architecture or performance
- Prefer Web Frontend over Technical Concept or System Design when the topic is clearly frontend-specific (e.g. “Angular performance improvisation”)

Security / Cloud / DevOps / Database / etc.
- Use the specific category when the question clearly belongs there.

Common misclassification rules (STRICT):
- “Angular performance improvisation” → Web Frontend or Technical Concept (NEVER System Design)
- “HLD / High Level Design / HM round design” → System Design (NEVER Coding)
- “One Tree question” / “Tree question” / “Graph traversal based question” → Coding
- “closure coding snippet” / “Promise vs observables with coding” with no actual code → Technical Concept
- Short classic problem names (“Parking Lot”, “LRU cache”, “Rate Limiter”) → LLD or Coding depending on context, never System Design unless explicitly high-level architecture
</question_type_guidelines>

<coding_classification>
ALL DSA, algorithm, LeetCode, and data-structure coding problems MUST use questionType = “Coding”. Never use DSA, ALGORITHM, LEETCODE, or DATA_STRUCTURE as questionType. DSA categories may appear in topics ONLY when explicitly supported by the source.
</coding_classification>

<normalization_rules>
Normalize wording only when doing so does NOT change meaning or granularity.
Examples:
“Asked to implement LRU.” → “Implement an LRU cache.”
“They asked me to find the longest substring without repeating characters.” → “Find the longest substring without repeating characters.”
“Asked to design a URL shortener.” → “Design a URL shortener.”
DO NOT add algorithms, data structures, constraints, input/output assumptions, complexity requirements, scale, traffic, availability, APIs, architecture components, technologies, or follow-up questions unless explicitly stated in the source.
Do not make a question more sophisticated than the source.
</normalization_rules>

<question_fidelity>
Preserve original technical meaning and granularity.
If the source provides only a short question, keep it short. If detailed requirements are present, preserve them.
Do not generalize a specific question. Do not specialize a vague question. Do not convert a topic or answer into a question. Do not infer missing details.
</question_fidelity>

<description_rules>
questionDescription is an ELABORATED VERSION of questionText. It explains the SAME question more clearly and completely so a developer can understand it without reading the surrounding experience.
- Always present and non-empty.
- Describe the problem itself, not the interview, candidate, or interviewer.
- Stay strictly within the scope of the CURRENT question.
- Use only details explicitly supported by the source and belonging to this question.
- Do not import requirements, constraints, examples, inputs, outputs, technologies, or details from neighboring questions.
- Do not invent examples, constraints, or requirements.
- Do not solve the question or add recommended approaches.
- Make the description independently understandable.
- Avoid phrases such as “The candidate is asked…”, “The interviewer asks…”, “This tests…”.
A short source question may have a short description. A detailed source question should have a correspondingly detailed description. Source fidelity is more important than length.
</description_rules>

<description_scope_rules>
For each extracted question:
1. Identify the exact problem represented by questionText.
2. Elaborate only that problem.
3. Use only technical details belonging to that question.
4. Never copy requirements, constraints, examples, inputs, outputs, technologies, or details from another question.
5. Never merge neighboring questions.
6. Never broaden or specialize a question using nearby content.
Each questionDescription has its own evidence boundary. Only an explicit cross-reference where the CURRENT question clearly depends on an earlier part of the SAME source may be used.
</description_scope_rules>

<description_elaboration_rules>
A good description may clarify what the input/problem is, what must be determined/computed/designed/implemented/explained, explicit requirements/constraints, explicit input/output behavior, explicit examples, explicit edge cases, and explicit follow-ups belonging to the same question.
If the source contains a useful example, preserve it. If the source does not contain an example, do NOT invent one.
</description_elaboration_rules>

<topics_rules>
Return 1–5 concise technical topics directly supported by the source. Do not add topics merely because they are commonly associated with the problem. For Coding questions, DSA categories may appear in topics only when explicitly supported by the source; questionType remains Coding.
</topics_rules>

<source_fidelity>
Each question must be independently grounded in the source. Never combine unrelated sentences to construct a new question. Never attach metadata or URLs from one question to another.

<duplicate_rules>
Do not return duplicate questions. Questions representing the same underlying interview problem, even with slightly different wording, appear only once. Keep the most faithful representation. Do not merge genuinely different questions merely because they are related.
</duplicate_rules>

<confidence_rules>
Assign confidence based ONLY on strength of evidence in the source (0.0–1.0).
0.90–1.00: exact question/task with essentially no ambiguity.
0.80–0.89: clearly describes the question/task with minor ambiguity.
0.70–0.79: clearly indicates a question/task but provides limited detail.
Below 0.70: reject the question.
Do not inflate confidence because a question is common, resembles a known LeetCode problem, or is technically plausible. Confidence reflects source evidence, not model recognition. Questions with confidence < 0.50 are ineligible.
</confidence_rules>

<granularity_rules>
questionGranularity measures how much concrete, usable detail the source itself provides about the question.
It is a continuous score from 0.0 to 1.0.

Purpose:
- High-granularity questions can be shown directly to users as standalone questions.
- Low-granularity questions remain part of the interview experience only and should not be promoted as standalone questions.

Bands:

0.90 – 1.00  High granularity
  The source provides a clear, self-contained problem with useful extra detail such as:
  - explicit requirements or constraints
  - input/output examples
  - actual code snippet
  - concrete expected behavior
  - multiple parts or follow-ups that belong to the same question
  - an exact LeetCode / problem link
  Examples:
  - “Delete duplicate value key from an object – Input={a:1, b:2, c:3, d:1} Output={a:1, b:2, c:3}”
  - “Find the longest substring that occurs at least twice (non-overlapping)”
  - “Design a Payment Service” + follow-ups about Kafka streams, partner bank failures, retries, transaction handling
  - Any question that includes an exact problem URL

0.78 – 0.89  Medium-High granularity
  The source states a clear, recognizable problem name (classic coding / design problem) with little or no extra detail.
  This is the correct band for short but unambiguous problem titles.
  Examples:
  - “Koko Eating Bananas”
  - “Longest Palindromic Substring”
  - “Find kth largest element”
  - “LLD: Parking Lot”
  - “Design a URL shortener”
  - “Next greatest element in circular array”
  - “Database Design: Books and Authors Store”

0.60 – 0.77  Medium granularity
  The source clearly indicates a technical question but the wording is somewhat short or only partially specific.
  Examples:
  - “Implement debounce in JavaScript”
  - “Async vs Observable in Angular”
  - “Time complexity of map and unordered_map”
  - “Promise vs observables with coding”
  - “Design a caching strategy for a heavy data dashboard” (no further detail)

0.45 – 0.59  Low-Medium granularity
  The source only gives a vague or topic-style mention. Not enough to form a good standalone question.
  Examples:
  - “Event loop”
  - “closure coding snippet” (no actual snippet shown)
  - “Angular performance improvisation”
  - “Some problem regarding sweep line algorithm”
  - “GenAI questions”
  - “LP questions (conflict, decision, initiative)”
  - “Security of the application”

0.00 – 0.44  Low granularity
  Extremely vague or generic. Normally reject or keep only inside the experience.
  Examples:
  - “Discussed databases”
  - “Some DSA questions”
  - “HLD round”
  - “One Tree question”

CRITICAL RULES:
- Score based ONLY on the detail present in the source.
- Do NOT raise the score just because the problem is well-known or classic.
- A clear, recognizable problem name (even without extra constraints) belongs in the 0.78–0.89 band — do NOT drop it to 0.50.
- Only pure topic-style or highly vague mentions belong below 0.60.
- When the source adds useful follow-ups, constraints, or examples to a problem, move it into the 0.90+ band.
- Never return values outside [0.0, 1.0].
</granularity_rules>
